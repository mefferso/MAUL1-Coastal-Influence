# MAUL1 local forcing feasibility and significant-rise research

**Status: research / shadow evaluation / not operational.** This is a source-feasibility result, not a predictor evaluation. No forecast, verification rule, frozen coefficient, or dashboard behavior was changed.

## Executive finding

Local forcing remains a plausible explanation for some misses, but the available records reviewed here do not establish that explanation. PR #55's reproducible audit found 109 outcome-defined, grouped 24-hour rises of at least 0.50 ft, 28 detected and 81 missed; 57 missed groups had maximum predicted rise below 0.40 ft, 22 were between 0.40 and 0.50 ft, and two crossed the forecast threshold only after their first qualifying target. These are target-defined groups, not verified independent physical flood events.

The Phase 5B archive has no local precipitation or proposed tributary observations to join. Historical 00065 tributary stage is nominally listed from 2007 at the three Baton Rouge sites, but this runner could not access USGS endpoints. The nearby rainfall series at those sites is explicitly operational and temporary, with a 120-day display/retention limit and quality limitations that USGS says make the data unsuitable for archival-grade scientific interpretation. The separate 301828090363100 precipitation page lists a longer 2021–2026 period, but actual retrieval of older rows and timestamp-level completeness were not verified. NOAA MRMS supplies the strongest prospective rainfall candidate: a roughly 1-km, hourly multi-sensor estimate can be aggregated over a delineated upstream drainage area, but full historical archive completeness and actual receipt latency still require measurement.

**Result:** none of the candidate sources has demonstrated precursor skill against the 109 groups. Archive watershed-aggregated MRMS prospectively, and capture the USGS stage and rainfall records in a separate research archive with explicit quality states. Do not begin a rise-focused model experiment until availability, hydrologic routing and event-blocked comparisons are established.

## Historical relationship diagnostics

The incorporated failure audit covers 32,406 completed 24-hour holdout rows with target times from 2023-01-07 through 2026-10-20. It contains MAUL1 and the existing DENL1/NWCL1/MDLL1 features but no precipitation or the proposed tributary series. Thus:

- **Candidate observations joined to rises, misses, false alerts or nonresponse controls: 0.** This means no candidate data were present in the committed archive; it does not mean no rain or tributary response occurred.
- **The 109 groups cannot be matched to local USGS rainfall from the existing archive.** The short retention limit for the three local operational rainfall series also prevents reliable historical reconstruction. The longer period shown for 301828090363100 needs a direct date-bounded API retrieval and gap audit before it can support an event analysis.
- **Tributary stage could be informative but was not joined.** Stage-only changes, if eventually analyzed, must stay in native local datum and be interpreted with rating/control/backwater information. No discharge conversion is justified from the reviewed pages.
- **No lead/lag result, event/nonresponse contrast, effect size, confidence interval, or seasonal robustness result is estimable from the delivered files.** Retrospective valid time alone cannot show when an observation reached an operational forecaster.
- The current archive lacks local predictor receipt times and quality history, and hourly forecast target windows overlap. A future comparison should summarize at event/episode blocks, use separate nonresponse control windows, stratify by season and chronology, and retain missingness rather than fill it.

The 2023+ evaluation period has already been reused for model evaluation. It can support descriptive matched source diagnostics, but any model-selection claim requires a later prospective period or another truly untouched chronological block.

## Reproducible package and limits

`usgs_acquisition.py` queries only a small allowlist of USGS site/parameter pairs and a bounded recent window. It preserves raw GeoJSON feature properties, including `time`, `value`, `unit_of_measure`, `qualifier`, `approvals_status` and `last_modified`, and records request/receipt time and a response SHA-256. Provider errors and empty results receive separate statuses. It does not normalize datums, interpolate, backfill, or connect to a forecast. `coverage.py` reports observed timestamps, gaps and qualifiers without filling values, and filters strictly to observations before the explicit issue time; the outcome target is retained as a label only.

The package was validated on synthetic fixtures because the workspace had no outbound network. This checks parsing and causal timestamp guards only; it is not evidence that live USGS calls work. No raw provider data, fabricated observations, or compressed historical archives are committed. MRMS watershed retrieval and geometry-weighted aggregation are documented as a subsequent step because the drainage polygon, grid-coordinate handling and source completeness must first be independently verified.

## Prospective collection recommendation

1. **Archive MRMS hourly QPE over the delineated upstream MAUL1 drainage area.** Store both Pass 1 and Pass 2 valid intervals where available, the MRMS product name/version, spatial grid and aggregation method, quality/gauge-influence context, source URL and hash, `valid_end_utc`, retrieval/receipt time, and `available_at_utc`. Store only area-weighted hourly watershed totals and coverage fractions, not whole national GRIB files. Confirm the drainage polygon against the gage location and river network before using it.
2. **Capture recent USGS observations in a separate research-only store** for 301828090363100 00045 and the three station 00045/00065 series. Preserve provider qualifiers, approval status, unit, time-series ID, datum/parameter metadata, original response and a retrieval timestamp. Label station rainfall “operational/temporary” until the provider confirms otherwise. Keep missing, delayed, invalid and retrieval-error states distinct.
3. **Do not overwrite earlier capture evidence.** Use immutable daily or hourly snapshots, content hashes, bounded retention, and graceful workflow failure. A dedicated scheduled GitHub Action can run at a practical cadence at no infrastructure cost; it should be added only after a live API smoke retrieval and Actions fixture test. The current package deliberately adds no scheduled workflow because the live endpoint could not be verified from this network-restricted workspace.

## Decision and next milestone

- **A — Future model experiment:** not yet. First demonstrate that MRMS catchment rainfall and/or an interpretable tributary stage signal is available before issue time and adds event-level information beyond DENL1 and the existing coastal network. Freeze the hypotheses and scorekeeping before fitting anything.
- **B — Prospective archive:** yes. MRMS watershed aggregates are the priority. Capture the USGS series in shadow as context with their quality and datum limitations.
- **C — Physical-process research:** yes. Delineate contributing watersheds, establish tributary routing/travel times, inspect channel controls and floodgate/backwater influence, and reconcile gage datum histories before interpreting stage response.
- **D — Reject or restrict sources:** reject 00065-as-discharge for all three tributaries; restrict 07378746 stage as evidence of upstream tributary inflow because of its downstream boundary location; restrict the 120-day USGS precipitation series to provisional context. None is rejected from raw prospective collection if labeled correctly. Do not interpret USGS 00065 against the 4.0-ft NAVD88 threshold.

**Next research milestone:** retrieve at least two complete wet seasons of MRMS basin aggregates prospectively, quantify valid-to-receipt latency and missingness, and obtain a verified catchment/routing description. Then perform a predeclared descriptive comparison of missed, detected, false-alert and seasonal nonresponse episodes using only source rows that demonstrably arrived before each issue time. Advance to an isolated challenger only if the data are sufficiently complete and physically relevant and a blocked comparison shows incremental precursor information beyond DENL1/coastal inputs without relying on the reused 2023+ tuning period.

No operational forecasting or coastal advisory recommendation is made.

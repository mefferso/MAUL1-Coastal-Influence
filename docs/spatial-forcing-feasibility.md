# MAUL1 Local Forcing Feasibility: Expanded Coastal Observation Network

**Research memo — 25 October 2026**  
**Status: feasibility and prospective archive recommendation only; not forecast inputs.**  
**Scope:** local rainfall and tributaries; Lake Pontchartrain wind field; spatial lake, pass, and river water levels. The map is [downloadable as HTML](../site/data/research/spatial-network/network-map.html) with its [station GeoJSON](../site/data/research/spatial-network/network-stations.geojson).

## Executive finding

The current five-station context (DENL1, NWCL1, MDLL1, MCHL1, MAUL1) is not enough to establish whether winds and spatial water-level gradients are represented across Lake Pontchartrain, Lake Maurepas, and Pass Manchac. NWCL1's wind is valuable at New Canal, but it is a shore/structure measurement, not an observed lake-wide wind field. The project's CO-OPS/NDBC NWCL1 comparison is a duplicate re-report: 86,046 overlapping hourly values have mean direction difference 0.308 degrees and mean speed difference 0.029 knots, with averaging disabled. It supports parser agreement, not an independent wind vector. [Project comparison](../site/data/history/wind-comparison.json)

There is a promising historical open-water candidate, NDBC LKPL1 in western Lake Pontchartrain, at 30.315, -90.281, with a documented 13 m anemometer and standard meteorological archive listed for 2004–2012. The listed historical span does not establish current service, complete coverage, or real-time availability. NOAA CO-OPS Frenier Landing (8762484/FREL1) has historical wind/meteorological products, but NDBC reports no recent observations and meteorological history ends in 2021; CO-OPS product evidence reaches 2023-03-31. Classify FREL1 as historical-only and do not prioritize prospective acquisition unless a genuinely current source is independently established. Neither candidate presently supplies a verified continuous current lake-wide wind record.

Spatial levels can add context if datum and sensor epochs are controlled. The USGS 073802302 Pass Manchac series is 15-minute gage height from May 2022 to September 2026 in the current inventory; in the two reviewed project comparison windows, the native series match exactly at 373 valid times (20–24 Dec 2022) and 479 valid times (14–18 Nov 2024), 852 paired times total. The first window has only 373 of 480 expected MCHL1 readings (77.7%); the second has 479 of 480 (99.8%). That is strong evidence of duplicate reporting in those windows, but does not prove instrument identity, vertical datum, or QC. Count it as one Pass signal, keep MCHL1 shadow-only, and do not stack the feeds as independent predictors. [Overlap evidence](../site/data/research/mchl1-usgs-overlap/comparison.json)

No additional station's incremental predictive value has been established. Among the five NWPS gauges reviewed, none is verified as currently reporting as of 2026-10-25; LRLL1 and WEGL1 have stale genuine observations (Oct 13 and Oct 12 respectively), while PLAL1/RGTL1/RFPL1 remain unverified. FREL1 is historical-only; BCFL1 current activity is unverified. The Phase 3A history comprises 130 monthly hourly files spanning January 2016 through October 2026 (4,137,183 station/variable rows) queried over full UTC calendar months; valid observations, missing intervals, and source availability still vary, and the hourly table is not a raw provider receipt log. It does not include the unacquired spatial candidates inventoried here. Phase-specific event windows and the two MCHL1/USGS source-comparison windows are separate, narrower evidence sets. Start timestamped prospectively archiving the sources below. [Phase 7 data-quality record](phase7-data-quality.md) · [monthly coverage metadata](../site/data/history/coverage.json). Keep Phase 6 frozen and unchanged.

**Current status conclusion:** none of these five gauges meets the strict verified-current criterion as of 25 October 2026. LRLL1 and WEGL1 have authentic, dated NWPS observations in the preceding two weeks, but those are stale relative to hourly/15-minute feeds and do not verify current telemetry. PLAL1, RGTL1, and RFPL1 lack a located recent observation timestamp. This is a verification limit, not proof that the instruments are shut down. NWPS display/API is free and sustainable for current observations; its lack of a general archive means prospective archival should store raw provider payload, observation timestamp, receipt timestamp, source ID, units, datum metadata and QC flags. HADS feeds are provisional and distinguish sample interval from hourly transmit interval.

### Proposed minimal expanded observation network

Keep the existing five-station context exactly as currently defined. Add **(a)** an hourly/daily archival of MRMS QPE over fixed, documented Lake Maurepas/Pass Manchac contributing areas and local lake-adjacent cells; **(b)** one near-target Amite/HADS stream at PVLL1, retaining PC precipitation and HG stage (plus other reported elements) with both valid and hourly receipt times; **(c)** a western-lake NOAA wind/rain site only after proving active telemetry (FREL1 is historical-only; LKPL1 historical only; BCFL1 current status unverified); **(d)** one independently validated western-lake stage site, choosing after audit among LRLL1, PLAL1 or CO-OPS/USACE options; and **(e)** one Pass Manchac channel-level stream, preferably USGS 073802302 if the sensor/datum metadata pass review, with MCHL1 retained shadow-only until independently resolved. This is a minimum for testing, not model inclusion.

Do not initially add WEGL1 to the minimum because it is about 0.7 km from NWCL1 and likely spatially redundant. Do not add PLAL1 until current data/datum are established. Keep RGTL1/RFPL1 as a single controlled Rigolets comparison pair; select at most one only after paired archive and datum review. Do not initially add the Mandeville 85575LA level because MDLL1 already supplies central/northern lake context. Preserve both as controlled redundancy candidates. FREL1 is historical-only and BCFL1 remains unverified; archive NWCL1 plus documented nearby ASOS and MRMS without claiming a lake-wide wind field; publish the coverage gap.

## Wind and spatial hypotheses to preregister

Meteorological direction is “from”; convert each raw wind to east/north transport components before averaging, never average degrees. Use vector means/integrals over completed 3-, 6-, 12-, and 24-hour windows as exploratory candidates, plus speed-weighted persistence, directional-shift episodes, and station contrasts grouped by north/south/east/west/over-water/shore/airport/structure exposure. Define the lake fetch axis and Pass Manchac/channel orientation from mapped geometry before testing; project `u,v` onto those axes with a declared sign convention. Retain station and height-specific series. A 10 m-equivalent conversion or wind-stress proxy is only a sensitivity analysis for known height, exposure and roughness; report the drag/air-density assumptions and do not use airport or unknown-height wind as open-water stress.

For water levels, test each station’s native differences and tendencies first. A spatial difference of absolute elevations is allowed only within a certified datum/benchmark/sensor epoch, with the transformation source cited. Otherwise compare standardized within-station changes, explicitly labeled; do not subtract arbitrary native gage heights. Hypotheses are: western-lake setup precedes target response; lake spatial gradients reflect wind-driven redistribution; Pass Manchac changes precede or accompany MAUL1; persistent winds show delayed response; similar NWCL1 conditions yield different MAUL1 changes when spatial context differs; and local basin rain/tributary flow coincides with elevated downstream lake levels. These are physically testable associations, not causal conclusions.

## Incremental-information evaluation protocol

1. **Freeze target definitions and candidates before joining.** Use the existing Phase 8 event/lead protocol and its event IDs. Candidate source set above is fixed for first evaluation; no station/lag/window search driven by full-sample outcomes. Exploratory 3/6/12/24-hour windows are reported as a prespecified family with multiplicity-aware or descriptive-only inference, not a picked “best” window.
2. **Required comparisons:** detected MAUL1 rises; underpredicted rises relative to the existing authentic baseline where an eligible verification period exists; false-alert episodes; and matched nonresponse controls. Keep event/control episode as the sampling unit; do not inflate `n` with hourly rows. Report coverage and eligible event counts per candidate and joint complete-case coverage.
3. **Incremental baseline:** compare each predeclared spatial variable against information already in NWCL1, MDLL1, DENL1 and MAUL1, with time-respecting/event-blocked chronological folds, whole-event grouping and a temporal embargo at least as long as the predictor lookback/target horizon. Report redundancy/correlation only on training folds, then held-out incremental metrics and uncertainty. A candidate with few events or poor joint coverage is inconclusive, not rejected or promoted.
4. **Holdout protection:** 2023+ historical holdout has already been reused in project analyses; it cannot serve as independent confirmatory evidence after feature selection. Label any inspection there exploratory. Confirmatory improvement requires future archived events or an untouched chronological block identified before candidate fitting. Do not retrain or modify frozen Phase 6 baseline forecasts, model inputs, thresholds, issuance or verification records.
5. **False alerts and nonresponse:** explicitly check whether spatial features separate actual responses from high-wind/high-level false alerts and from similar forcing episodes without MAUL1 response. Report event-level calibration/skill only if sample size and coverage support it; otherwise provide case tables and uncertainty, without promoting stations.
6. **Lead accounting:** report (i) valid-time precursor lead (observation timestamp to target onset), (ii) physical propagation time as a separate mechanistic interpretation requiring independent evidence, and (iii) usable forecast lead after observation receipt, QC, completed-bin availability, persistence confirmation and processing. Existing archives generally lack provider receipt times, so they cannot prove usable operational lead. From prospective archives, record provider timestamp/receipt and local retrieval time separately.
7. **Missingness and redundancy:** compute interval availability per event/station, longest gaps, outage co-occurrence, and pairwise residual redundancy after existing five-station inputs. Compare sensors sharing station IDs/telemetry as a single source unless operators document otherwise.

Do not claim that correlation or lead-lag diagnostics establish physical causality. A rise observed first at a candidate is an observed sequence, not proof that water physically propagated from that station to MAUL1.

## Data provenance and limits

This memo was checked against source-owner documentation and the project archive on 25 October 2026. NOAA, USGS, USACE and CRMS products can have revised metadata or records; recheck their current station pages before operational acquisition. Candidate station locations on the map are provider coordinates when cited; Causeway geometry and connecting arrows are illustrative context. Map markers indicate sites, never continuous spatial coverage.

The repository contains a full-month-query historical core archive (130 hourly monthly files, 4,137,183 rows, January 2016–October 2026), plus event-specific analysis windows and prospective captures. The monthly coverage denominator is explicit, but missing hourly observations, retrieval failures, source limitations, and absent spatial candidate feeds mean it is not complete continuous coverage. This feasibility review does not claim a measured improvement, independently validate a rise lead, or authorize any extra predictor in the forecast. The existing five-station network, frozen Phase 6 baseline, authentic issuance records, verification records, and dashboard functionality remain unchanged.
